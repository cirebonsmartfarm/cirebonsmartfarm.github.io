The two things that decide market quality have to start days before the fish come out of the pond.

Harvest Starts Several Days Before the Fish Come Out

What you do in the last days sets the selling price

Many farmers treat harvest as a one-day job. But the two things that matter most for quality, purging and removing off-flavor, have to start long before the fish come out.

The exact same fish can sell as different quality just because of how they were handled in the last few days.

Purging

Emptying the gut before the fish are transported

Purging means fasting the fish before harvest. The point is not to save feed, it is to empty their gut.

Fish with a full stomach keep passing waste during transport. That waste turns into ammonia in the cramped transport water, and it is the ammonia that kills fish on the way. An empty stomach also lowers oxygen use.

Common purging times
Purpose Purging time Note
Harvest and sell on site 12 to 24 hours Enough to empty the gut
Short-distance transport 24 hours Transport water stays cleaner
Long-distance transport 24 to 36 hours The longer the trip, the more it matters
Fingerling transport 12 to 24 hours Fingerlings are more fragile, do not overdo it

Do not overdo it

Purging for more than two days starts to cut weight and leaves the fish weak. The point is to empty the gut, not to slim the fish down. During purging, keep the aeration running and keep the water clean.

Removing Off-Flavor

The problem that lowers the price most often, and the one most misunderstood

Off-flavor in freshwater fish does not come from dirt, and it cannot be washed off. It is caused by two compounds called geosmin and MIB, produced by cyanobacteria and actinomycetes in the water.

Both compounds enter through the gills, then build up in the fish's fat tissue because they are fat-soluble. Uptake is fast, only a few hours. Clearing them out is far slower, it takes days.

  1. Move the fish into clean flowing water

    The water has to be free of algae and organic matter. Moving them into water that smells just as bad will clear nothing.

  2. Do not feed while the process runs

    Besides emptying the gut, not feeding keeps the purging water clean so the off-flavor compounds are not taken up again.

  3. Allow three to ten days

    How long depends on the water temperature and how fatty the fish is. Cooler water slows it down, fatty fish hold it longer. In bad cases it can take more than ten days.

  4. Test by cooking a sample, do not guess

    This is the method the industry uses and there is no substitute for it. Take one fish, cook it without any seasoning, then taste it. If it still smells earthy, keep going. Your nose is more sensitive than any instrument you can afford.

  5. Prevent it from the start by keeping algae down

    Shading the pond, not overfeeding, and keeping the siphoning routine is far cheaper than purging for days at the end of the cycle.

Harvest and Transport

Whether the fish arrive alive is decided here

  1. Harvest in the morning while it is still cool

    Cool water holds more oxygen and the fish are calmer. Avoid the heat of midday completely.

  2. Lower the pond water in stages, not all at once

    A sudden drop makes the fish panic, injure each other, and lose their slime coat. Lower it slowly while herding the fish to one side.

  3. Wet every tool before it touches the fish

    Dry nets, buckets, and scales strip off the protective slime coat. Lost slime is the way in for fungus, and that shows up as white patches a few days later, in the buyer's hands.

  4. Grade by size while you harvest

    One size per container is easier to sell and buyers value it more. This is work that adds no cost at all.

  5. Set transport density by distance and temperature

    The longer the trip and the hotter the weather, the fewer fish per container. Give aeration or pure oxygen for trips longer than one hour.

  6. Lower the transport water temperature a little

    Cooler water slows the fish's breathing rate, so the oxygen lasts longer and less ammonia forms. Bring it down slowly, not with ice straight in.

Count again after harvest, do not just count the money

Record how many fish actually came out, their total weight, and the total feed used. From that you get your real survival rate and FCR. Those two numbers are what tell you what to fix in the next cycle, and without a record, the next cycle just repeats the same mistakes.

Questions About This Module

What participants ask most often

How do you remove off-flavor in fish?

Move the fish into clean flowing water and give them no feed for three to ten days. Off-flavor comes from geosmin and MIB, compounds produced by cyanobacteria that enter through the gills and build up in the fish's fat tissue.

Because it sits inside the flesh, this smell cannot be removed by washing or by seasoning. The only way to test is to cook one sample fish without seasoning and taste it. If it still smells earthy, keep purging.

What is the purpose of purging fish before transport?

To empty the gut. Fish with a full stomach keep passing waste during the trip, that waste turns into ammonia in the cramped transport water, and it is the ammonia that kills the fish before they arrive.

An empty stomach also lowers oxygen use. Allow 12 to 24 hours for short distances and 24 to 36 hours for long ones. More than two days starts to cut weight and leaves the fish weak.

How long until catfish are ready to harvest?

Usually 60 to 90 days, from 5 to 7 centimeter fingerlings to table size, depending on stocking density, feed quality, water temperature, and how disciplined the grading is.

Tilapia takes longer, around four to six months to reach 250 to 350 grams. Routine grading shortens the time to harvest, because the small fish are not left behind by the big ones.

How do you transport live fish so they do not die on the way?

Purge them for 24 hours first, harvest in the morning, and bring the transport water temperature down a little, slowly, so the fish breathe slower. Set the density to match the distance: the farther and the hotter, the fewer fish per container.

For trips longer than one hour, provide aeration or pure oxygen. Wet every tool before it touches the fish, because a stripped protective slime coat turns into fungus a few days later, in the buyer's hands.

What should you record after harvest?

Three numbers. How many fish actually came out against how many were stocked, which gives you the survival rate. The total harvest weight. And the total feed used over the whole cycle.

From those three you get your real survival rate and FCR, and those two numbers are what tell you what needs fixing. Without a record, the next cycle will just repeat the same mistakes without you noticing.
