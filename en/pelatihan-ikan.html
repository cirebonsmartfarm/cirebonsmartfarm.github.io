What We Share Here

Working notes from the field, not theory copied out of a book

Fish farming at high stocking density fails not because of too little feed, but because of numbers that are never measured. Ammonia read without pH, aeration sized from fish count alone, and grading that keeps getting put off.

The seven modules below work through those numbers one by one, with the formulas, safe-range tables, and worked examples you can copy straight away.

Fully open

No locked modules, no registration form, no request for your phone number.

Numbers, not guesswork

Every recommendation comes with its working range, its unit, and the reason behind it.

Easy to read on a phone

Every table and diagram adapts to a small screen, so you can open it while standing in the field.

Free to use for teaching

Copy it for farmer groups, school modules, or internal training.

The Learning Order We Suggest

If you are truly starting from zero

  1. Master the water first

    Module 1. Almost every failure starts here, and almost all of it can be prevented if you get used to measuring before anything gets sick.

  2. Understand where the ammonia goes

    Module 2. This is what sets biofloc apart from an ordinary pond, along with how to calculate the molasses dose correctly.

  3. Make sure the aeration can carry it

    Module 3. High stocking density without enough aeration only delays the loss.

  4. Only then stock the fingerlings

    Module 4. Once the pond is ready, then you choose fingerlings, acclimate them, and set a grading schedule.

  5. Manage the feed and watch the health

    Module 5 and Module 6 run alongside each other through the whole cycle.

  6. Finish it properly

    Module 7. The two things that decide the selling price have to start days before harvest.

Equipment You Need to Prepare

These four decide the outcome, the rest can come later

DO meter

The most decisive tool in fish farming. Oxygen is the one thing that can kill a whole pond overnight.

pH meter and ammonia test kit

Ammonia must never be read without pH beside it. The two always go in pairs.

Imhoff Cone

For measuring floc volume. Without it, managing biofloc is just guessing from the color of the water.

Scale and sampling net

The feed dose is worked out from the actual weight of the fish, and that weight can only be known by weighing.

Start small, but measure seriously

One pond or ten planting holes with neat records teaches far more than a large operation run on guesswork. It is the daily log that later tells you what changed when something goes wrong.

Questions About This Training

Before you start reading

Is this fish farming training really free?

Genuinely free. No cost, no need to register, and no module is locked. All of the material is open and can be read from a phone at the pond edge.

We opened it because a pond managed properly benefits everyone, including the people who buy fish in Cirebon.

Which fish is this material for?

Mainly tilapia and catfish, the two most widely farmed freshwater species in Indonesia. Most of the principles also apply to pangasius, gourami, and tambaqui.

What you need to adjust are the stocking density figures and the tolerance for low oxygen, because those two differ a lot between species. Catfish, for example, tolerate two to three times the density tilapia can.

Can it be used for an ordinary tarpaulin pond rather than biofloc?

Yes. The modules on water quality, aeration, fingerlings and grading, feed and FCR, disease, and harvest apply to every pond system.

The only biofloc-specific one is Module 2 on the nitrogen cycle and the carbon calculation. It is still worth reading, because it explains where ammonia goes in any pond.

What is the minimum equipment I need?

A DO meter, a pH meter, an ammonia test kit, an Imhoff cone if you use biofloc, and a scale for sampling. Plus a blower that is big enough, along with backup power for it.

The DO meter is the most decisive one. Oxygen is the only thing that can empty a whole pond overnight, and no symptom shows up early enough to save it if you are not measuring.

Is there a certificate after finishing the modules?

No. This is open reading material, not a certified training program, and we do not want to suggest otherwise.

What you can take home is the material itself. Copy it to teach groups of fish farmers or to put together extension material, as long as the source is credited.
