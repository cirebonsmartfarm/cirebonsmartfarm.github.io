Flowers Grown to Be Eaten

A small, high-value crop for restaurants and patisseries

Edible flowers are used as plate garnish in restaurants, cafes, and cake shops. Demand is small but steady, the price per gram is far above leafy greens, and there are still few competitors in Indonesia.

What makes them different from ordinary vegetables is one hard rule. Because the flowers are eaten raw, no pesticide may touch the part that gets harvested. All pest control has to be done through hygiene and managing the environment.

Zinnia

The longest productive life, about eight months after transplant. Many colors and thick petals.

Marigold

Flowers quickly, ready to transplant at fifteen days old. Productive life is about three months.

Sweet William

Longest in the nursery, about thirty days, but its productive life can reach a year.

Torenia

Small bluish-purple flowers. Ready to transplant at about one month old, productive for about three months.

Yellow cosmos

The fastest, ready to transplant at fifteen days old, but its productive life is only about two months.

Growing media used

Unsifted fibrous cocopeat, or a mix of cocopeat and worm castings.

Sowing Edible Flowers

The principle is the same as for vegetables, but it demands more hygiene

  1. Sterilize the media first

    Soak the media overnight in a dilute cleaning solution, then rinse until the water dripping out reads below 0.7 mS/cm. Because no pesticide may be used later, hygiene at the start is the only defense.

  2. One seed per cell, 128-cell tray

    All five flower species above use the same pattern. Flower seeds are generally small, so do not sow them too deep.

  3. Give a dark period

    Cover tightly and mist every day until the seeds break open. Let the sprouts stretch about half a centimeter before introducing them to light.

  4. Tend them on an ebb and flow system

    Fertigate once a day or as needed, at pH 5.8 to 6.0. Raise the concentration in stages from 1.0 to 1.2 at the true-leaf stage, 1.2 to 1.5 as the seedlings strengthen, then 1.8 to 2.3 in the last three days before transplant.

  5. Keep the leaves dry

    Do not water from above and do not overwater. Wet leaves on flower seedlings are very easily attacked by fungus.

Planting and Care

Either inside a greenhouse or in open beds

Two common planting methods
Stage Inside a greenhouse In open beds
Land preparation Sterilize the beds, netting, and floor Till the soil, spread leftover cocopeat as a soil conditioner
Layout Beds with drip line Ridges about one meter by fifty meters
Spacing between ridges Follows the bed width About 25 centimeters
Additional Re-sterilize every cycle Silver mulch and drip line with holes every 20 cm
Plant spacing About 20 centimeters About 20 centimeters

Fertigation starts in the morning, and the number of cycles is adjusted to the weather. Weeds are pulled regularly, because edible flowers are small and easily outcompeted.

The working concentration sits at EC 1.8 to 2.3 mS/cm with pH 5.8 to 6.2. The fertilizer mix rests on calcium nitrate, one high-phosphorus compound fertilizer to push flowering, and magnesium sulfate.

Phosphorus pushes flowers, nitrogen pushes leaves

If your flower plants are leafy but rarely flower, nitrogen is most likely too high. Increase the phosphorus and potassium share, then wait one to two weeks before judging the result.

Harvest and Handling

The part that most determines the selling price

  1. Harvest every day before the light gets harsh

    Morning is best. Flowers picked in the heat have already lost water and will wilt much faster.

  2. Pick with the flower stem

    Pinch and pick at the flower stem. Do not include leaves or shoots, because both speed up rot inside the packaging.

  3. Line the tray with tissue

    Line under the picked flowers and cover them too. Tissue absorbs the condensation that appears when the temperature changes.

  4. Do not mix species in one tray

    Different flower colors and shapes need different handling, and mixing them makes sorting harder.

  5. Pack wearing gloves and a mask

    The flowers are eaten raw, so handling follows ready-to-eat food standards, not raw vegetable standards.

  6. Use anti-fog containers

    Condensation on the container wall makes the petals soggy. Anti-fog containers keep them looking dry.

Shelf life of five to seven days

And only if the cold chain is never broken from the farm onward. That is why edible flowers sell best through direct delivery to regular customers, not through a long distribution chain.

Questions About This Module

What participants ask most often

What are edible flowers?

Flowers grown specifically to be eaten, usually as plate garnish in restaurants, cafes, and cake shops. The species commonly grown include zinnia, marigold, sweet william, torenia, and yellow cosmos.

Their value per gram is far above leafy greens, demand is steady although not large, and there are still few competitors in Indonesia. They work well as an extra crop on a farm that is already running.

Can edible flowers be sprayed with pesticide?

No, especially not on the part that will be harvested. Because the flowers are eaten raw, pesticide residue will not go away.

All pest control has to be done through prevention: sterilizing the media, a footbath at the entrance, insect traps for monitoring, and removing badly infested plants. This is why hygiene for edible flowers is far stricter than for ordinary vegetables.

How long after planting can edible flowers be harvested?

It depends on the species. Yellow cosmos and marigold are the fastest, ready to transplant at fifteen days old. Zinnia takes about twenty-one to twenty-five days, while torenia and sweet william take about thirty days.

Their productive life also differs widely. Sweet william can produce for about a year and zinnia for about eight months, while yellow cosmos lasts only about two months.

What is the shelf life of edible flowers after harvest?

Five to seven days, provided the cold chain is never broken from the farm onward. Beyond that the petals go soggy and the color fades.

Because the shelf life is short, edible flowers sell best directly to regular customers such as restaurants and cake shops, not through a long distribution chain.

Why are my flower plants leafy but rarely flowering?

Almost always because nitrogen is too high compared with phosphorus and potassium. Nitrogen drives leaf and stem growth, while phosphorus drives flower formation.

Increase the phosphorus and potassium share in the solution, then wait one to two weeks before judging the result. Also make sure the plants get enough light, because too much shade delays flowering too.
