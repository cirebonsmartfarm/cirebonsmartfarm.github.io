Module 3: Aeration and Oxygen

Why sizing aeration from fish count alone is the mistake that most often turns fatal.

The Fish Are Not the Only Ones Using Up the Oxygen

In a biofloc pond, the bacteria often use more than the fish do

This is the misunderstanding that most often turns fatal. Farmers work out the oxygen demand from the fish count alone, then fit whatever blower is at hand. But in a mature biofloc pond, the bacterial colony can use as much oxygen as the fish being raised, or even more.

On top of that there is organic matter breaking down on the bottom. All three breathe all night long and not one of them produces oxygen.

Fish

The bigger and the more numerous they are, the more they use. It rises sharply after feeding and when the water temperature is high.

Floc bacteria

They breathe twenty-four hours a day. The denser the floc, the more they use. This is the one that often gets forgotten.

Organic matter on the bottom

Leftover feed and dead floc breaking down. It piles up quietly until one night it uses up the oxygen.

Water temperature

Warm water holds less oxygen. At 32 degrees, the oxygen the water can hold is far below what it is at 26 degrees.

Reading the Signs Before It Is Too Late

The fish tell you, usually about an hour ahead

Low-oxygen symptoms by level
Oxygen level What you see in the fish Danger level
Above 5 mg/L Spread out evenly, eating eagerly Safe
4 to 5 mg/L Still normal, appetite slightly down Caution
3 to 4 mg/L Gathering near the aerator, feed left over Danger, act now
2 to 3 mg/L Hanging at the surface, mouths gasping Critical
Below 2 mg/L Swimming weakly, some starting to die A mass die-off is under way

If the fish are already gasping at the surface, you are about an hour late

The right move is not medicine, it is getting air in as fast as possible. Turn on every aerator, add the backup blower, spray water from a height to break the surface, and stop feeding altogether. If you have it, replace part of the water with fresh oxygenated water.

Sizing and Arranging the Aeration

The number of points matters more than the size of the blower

  1. Choose the blower from water volume, not pond area

    The common rule for biofloc at high stocking density is about 2 to 3 liters of air per minute for every cubic meter of water. A D3 pond with one meter of water holds about 7 cubic meters, so it needs about 15 to 21 liters per minute.

  2. Add more aeration points, do not rely on one big point

    Four small air stones spread out are far better than one big stone in the middle. What you want is not big bubbles, it is fine bubbles spread evenly and a current that turns the whole body of water.

  3. Place the air stones near the bottom, but not touching it

    About five to ten centimeters off the bottom. Resting on the bottom stirs the sludge up constantly, too high leaves the bottom layer out of the circulation.

  4. Aim the current so it circles, not so it fights itself

    In a round pond, arrange the aeration points so the water turns one way. That rotation is what carries the sediment to the middle and into the central drain pipe.

  5. Check the dead spots every week

    Measure the oxygen at the point where the current is weakest, usually at the edge opposite the blower. The reading at that point decides the fate of the pond, not the reading in the middle.

Backup power is not optional, it is a requirement

In a biofloc pond at high stocking density, a two-hour power cut at night is enough to lose everything in the pond. Get a battery-powered backup blower or a generator ready before you stock the fingerlings, not after the first incident. Test-run it once a month, because a generator that is never started usually fails exactly when it is needed.

Questions About This Module

What participants ask most often

What size blower does a biofloc pond need?

The common rule for biofloc at high stocking density is about 2 to 3 liters of air per minute for every cubic meter of water. A pond three meters across with one meter of water holds about 7 cubic meters, so it needs about 15 to 21 liters of air per minute.

Work it out from water volume, not from the pond surface area. And remember that in biofloc the bacteria also use as much oxygen as the fish or more, so do not size it from the fish count alone.

Why do the fish hang at the surface in the morning?

Because dissolved oxygen hits its low point just before dawn. All night the fish, the bacteria, and the algae all breathe with no photosynthesis adding oxygen.

If the fish are already gasping at the surface, the oxygen is probably below 3 mg/L and you are about an hour late. Turn on every aerator, stop feeding, and spray water from a height to break the surface. Once it is safe, add more aeration points so it does not happen again.

Can a biofloc pond run without an aerator?

No, not if you mean real biofloc. Floc is a living colony of bacteria that breathe, and those bacteria are the biggest oxygen user in the pond. As soon as the aeration stops, the oxygen is gone within hours, the floc dies and settles, and then the ammonia spikes.

What people call catfish without an aerator is really a pond at ordinary stocking density that relies on the catfish being able to take air from the surface. That can work, but the stocking density has to be far lower and the water has to be changed regularly.

How long does a pond last if the power goes out?

In biofloc at high stocking density, two hours at night is enough to lose everything in the pond. During the day there is a little more room because the algae are still producing oxygen, but it is still dangerous.

That is why a battery-powered backup blower or a generator is a requirement, not an option. Test-run it once a month. A generator that is never started usually fails exactly when it is needed.
